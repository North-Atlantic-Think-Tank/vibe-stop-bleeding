# Briefing: PBO's Budget 2025 Issues for Parliamentarians

*The Parliamentary Budget Officer identifies critical fiscal challenges facing Canada's 2025 budget, including deficit reduction targets, defense spending commitments, and mounting debt servicing costs.*

---

## Context

The Parliamentary Budget Officer (PBO) has released its pre-budget analysis identifying key fiscal issues that will shape Budget 2025. With Canada facing pressure to meet NATO defense commitments while managing a growing deficit, parliamentarians must navigate competing priorities amid rising debt servicing costs.

## Key Facts & Findings

### 1. **Deficit Reduction Challenge**
- The government committed to reducing the deficit to 1% of GDP by 2026-27
- Current fiscal trajectory shows significant challenges in meeting this target
- Program spending restraint will be critical to achieving deficit reduction goals
- The PBO projects meeting the target will require substantial policy adjustments

### 2. **Defense Spending Pressure**
- Canada committed to reaching NATO's 2% GDP defense spending target by 2032
- Current defense spending sits well below 2% of GDP
- Meeting this commitment requires approximately $18-20 billion in additional annual spending
- This represents one of the largest fiscal pressures facing the government

### 3. **Debt Servicing Costs Surge**
- Public debt charges have increased dramatically due to higher interest rates
- Debt servicing costs now represent one of the fastest-growing budget items
- These costs reduce fiscal flexibility for new program spending
- PBO estimates debt charges will continue rising in the near term

### 4. **Provincial Healthcare Transfers**
- Federal healthcare transfers to provinces remain a significant budget component
- Aging population demographics increase pressure on healthcare funding
- Provinces continue to advocate for increased federal health transfers
- Long-term sustainability of healthcare funding requires attention

### 5. **Economic Growth Concerns**
- Modest economic growth projections limit revenue growth potential
- Slower growth makes deficit reduction more challenging
- Global economic uncertainty adds risk to fiscal planning
- Productivity improvements essential for long-term fiscal health

### 6. **Climate and Infrastructure Investment**
- Government committed to significant climate action investments
- Infrastructure renewal requires sustained capital investment
- These commitments compete with deficit reduction goals
- Balancing environmental and fiscal objectives presents challenges

### 7. **Tax Policy Considerations**
- Revenue measures may be required to meet fiscal targets
- Tax policy changes could impact economic competitiveness
- International tax developments (OECD minimum tax) require consideration
- Distributional impacts of tax changes require careful analysis

## Data Highlights

- **Deficit Target**: 1% of GDP by 2026-27 (approximately $32-35 billion based on projected GDP)
- **Defense Spending Gap**: Approximately $18-20 billion annually needed to reach 2% GDP target
- **Debt Servicing**: Rising from historical lows, now consuming larger share of federal revenues
- **Healthcare Transfers**: Billions in committed increases over coming years

## Key Conclusions & Implications

### 1. **Fiscal Trade-offs Are Unavoidable**
The government faces difficult choices between competing priorities. Meeting NATO defense commitments while reducing the deficit and maintaining program spending will require either significant revenue increases, spending cuts in other areas, or relaxing fiscal targets.

### 2. **Debt Affordability Requires Attention**
Rising debt servicing costs are constraining fiscal flexibility. With interest rates elevated compared to the past decade, the government has less room for new spending initiatives without raising revenues or cutting existing programs.

### 3. **Economic Growth Is Critical**
Achieving fiscal targets depends heavily on economic performance. Productivity improvements and sustainable growth are essential to generate the revenues needed to meet commitments while maintaining fiscal sustainability.

### 4. **Long-term Planning Essential**
Many of the fiscal pressures (defense spending, healthcare, climate action) are multi-year commitments. Budget 2025 must establish a credible long-term fiscal framework that addresses these pressures sustainably.

### 5. **Transparency and Accountability Matter**
The PBO's analysis underscores the importance of clear fiscal planning and realistic projections. Parliamentarians and Canadians need transparent information about fiscal trade-offs and the costs of policy choices.
