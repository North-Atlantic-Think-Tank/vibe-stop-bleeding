# Briefing: Canada's 2025 Federal Budget - Key Measures and Economic Outlook

*Analysis of Canada's 2025 federal budget revealing major tax relief measures, housing investments, and economic projections amid fiscal challenges.*

---

## Executive Briefing: Budget 2025

**Context:** The Canadian government has released its 2025 federal budget, outlining significant fiscal measures aimed at addressing cost-of-living pressures, housing affordability, and economic growth amid challenging deficit projections.

---

## Key Facts & Findings

### 1. **Major Tax Relief Initiative**
- **GST/HST Holiday**: Two-month tax break on essential items including groceries, restaurant meals, children's clothing, and toys
- **Working Canadians Rebate**: $250 direct payment to 18.7 million Canadians who worked in 2023 and earned up to $150,000
- **Combined Impact**: Delivers up to $1.6 billion in immediate relief to Canadian households
- **Timeline**: Measures implemented between November 2024 and February 2025

### 2. **Housing Affordability Measures**
- **Investment Scale**: Multi-billion dollar commitment to increase housing supply
- **Target**: Build more homes faster to address Canada's housing crisis
- **Focus Areas**: Rental construction, first-time homebuyer support, and municipal infrastructure
- **Expected Impact**: Aims to ease pressure on rental and purchase markets

### 3. **Fiscal Position and Deficit**
- **Deficit Projection**: Significantly higher than previous estimates
- **Economic Headwinds**: Global uncertainty and domestic growth challenges cited
- **Debt Management**: Government maintaining focus on fiscal sustainability
- **Interest Costs**: Rising debt servicing costs impacting fiscal flexibility

### 4. **Economic Growth Projections**
- **GDP Outlook**: Moderate growth expected for 2025-2026
- **Inflation Target**: Bank of Canada continuing efforts toward 2% target
- **Employment**: Labour market showing resilience despite slowdown
- **Private Sector Consensus**: Budget incorporates independent economic forecasts

### 5. **Support for Vulnerable Populations**
- **Targeted Programs**: Enhanced support for low and middle-income Canadians
- **Social Safety Net**: Strengthening existing programs amid economic uncertainty
- **Food Security**: Recognition of grocery cost pressures on Canadian families
- **Child Care**: Continued investments in affordable early learning

### 6. **Business and Investment Climate**
- **Competitiveness**: Measures to support Canadian businesses
- **Innovation Focus**: Continued emphasis on technology and clean economy
- **Trade Considerations**: Navigating international economic relationships
- **Productivity Concerns**: Acknowledgment of Canada's productivity challenges

### 7. **Healthcare Investments**
- **Federal Transfers**: Continued health funding to provinces and territories
- **Dental Care**: Expansion of Canadian Dental Care Plan
- **Mental Health**: Targeted investments in mental health services
- **Long-term Care**: Support for aging population needs

## Conclusions & Implications

### 1. **Short-term Relief Priority**
The government is prioritizing immediate cost-of-living relief over long-term fiscal consolidation, reflecting political pressure and economic necessity. This approach provides rapid support but raises questions about fiscal sustainability.

### 2. **Housing Crisis Acknowledgment**
Significant housing investments signal government recognition that affordability has reached crisis levels. Success will depend on execution speed and coordination with provinces and municipalities.

### 3. **Deficit Concerns Persist**
Higher-than-expected deficit projections may limit future fiscal flexibility and could impact Canada's credit rating. The government faces a delicate balance between stimulus and fiscal prudence.

### 4. **Targeted vs. Universal Approach**
The $150,000 income threshold for rebates represents targeted relief rather than universal support, potentially leaving out some middle-class Canadians facing affordability challenges.

### 5. **Economic Uncertainty Ahead**
Moderate growth projections and acknowledgment of global headwinds suggest cautious optimism. Canadian households and businesses should prepare for continued economic volatility in 2025-2026.
