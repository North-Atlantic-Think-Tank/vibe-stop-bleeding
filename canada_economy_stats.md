# Canadian Economy Statistics 2024-2025: Comprehensive Analysis

## Executive Summary
This report provides detailed statistics on key Canadian economic indicators for 2024-2025, comparing them with 2023 data where available. The data reveals significant financial pressures on Canadian households and growing national challenges.

---

## 1. Credit Card Debt

### 2024 Data
- **Total credit card debt**: $124 billion CAD (Q4 2024)
- **Average per borrower**: $4,681 CAD (Q4 2024)
- **Per capita (among cardholders)**: ~$4,562-$4,787 CAD
- **Year-over-year growth**: +9.2% (2024 vs 2023)

### 2023 Comparison
- Average per borrower: $4,119 CAD (Q3 2023)
- Growth from 2023: +6.0% to 2024

### Per Second Calculation
- Annual increase: ~$11.4 billion
- **Increase per second**: ~$361 CAD/second

### Key Insights
- 31 consecutive months of year-over-year balance growth
- 64% of balances are revolving (carried month-to-month)
- 54% of Canadians have credit card debt
- Delinquency rates (90+ days past due): 0.93% in Q4 2024

## 2. Household Debt

### 2024-2025 Data
- **Total household debt**: $2.56 trillion CAD (Q4 2024)
- **Population**: ~41.6 million (2025)
- **Per capita household debt**: ~$61,538 CAD
- **Non-mortgage debt per consumer**: $21,931 CAD (exceeds pre-pandemic levels)
- **Debt-to-income ratio**: 171.9% of gross income (Q1 2025)
- **Debt-to-GDP ratio**: 99.58% (Q1 2025)

### 2023 Comparison
- Total household debt: ~$2.45 trillion CAD
- Debt-to-GDP: 100.39% (Q4 2024)
- **Growth rate**: +4.6% year-over-year

### Per Second Calculation
- Annual increase: ~$117.8 billion
- **Increase per second**: ~$3,736 CAD/second

### Key Insights
- Highest household debt among G7 countries
- Average net worth per household: ~$1 million
- Wealth concentration: Top 20% hold 68.1% of financial assets

## 4. Unemployment

### October 2025 Data
- **Unemployment rate**: 6.9% (down from 7.1% in September 2025)
- **Total unemployed**: ~1.5 million people
- **Labor force**: ~21.7 million

### 2024 Comparison
- December 2024: 6.7% unemployment rate
- Year-over-year increase: +1.2 percentage points from December 2023
- Number of unemployed increased by 276,000 (+22.2%) year-over-year in November 2024

### Per Second Calculation
Based on annual change (+167,000 unemployed from March 2024 to March 2025):
- **Increase per second**: ~5.3 people per second becoming unemployed

### Key Insights
- Long-term unemployment (27+ weeks) at 23.7%
- Youth unemployment (15-24): 14.2%
- Employment gains often part-time rather than full-time
- 1.8 million jobs (8.8% of employment) depend on US demand

## 5. Homelessness

### 2024-2025 Data
- **Point-in-time count (single night, 74 communities)**: 60,000 people (Fall 2024)
- **Estimated total (all communities)**: 67,000+ people
- **Annual homelessness estimate**: 150,000 to 300,000 people
- **Ontario estimate**: 234,000 people (1.5% of population)
- **Ontario shelters/streets**: 80,000+ people in 2024

### Breakdown by Location (Fall 2024)
- Shelters: 35,940 people
- Unsheltered: 17,088 people (including 4,982 in encampments)
- Transitional housing: 6,872 people

### 2022-2023 Comparison
- 56 communities saw **79% increase** since last count (2020-22)
- Toronto: 15,418 people (October 2024) vs. 7,300 (April 2021) - **111% increase**
- 25% more people in shelters/streets compared to two years ago

### Per Second Calculation
Using conservative estimate of 60,000 nightly:
- If 25% annual growth continues: ~15,000 additional people per year
- **Increase per second**: ~0.48 people/second

### Key Insights
- Rate: 26 per 10,000 population in counted communities
- 66.7% are male
- 33.1% identify as Indigenous
- Primary causes: insufficient income (41%), lack of affordable housing (41%), mental health (44%)

## 6. Food Insecurity

### 2024 Data
- **Total people affected**: 10 million Canadians (25.5% of population)
- **Children affected**: 2.5 million
- **Severe food insecurity**: 2.6 million people
- **Provincial range**: 13.8% (Quebec) to 30.9% (Alberta)
- **Territorial rates**: Nunavut 58.1%, NWT 22.2%, Yukon 12.8%

### 2023 Comparison
- Total affected: 8.7 million (22.9%)
- Children: 2.1 million
- **Growth rate**: +15% year-over-year

### Per Second Calculation
- Annual increase: ~1.3 million people
- **Increase per second**: ~41 people/second becoming food insecure

### Key Insights
- Highest in 20 years of monitoring
- Disproportionately affects: Black Canadians (46.7%), Indigenous peoples off-reserve (39.9%), recent immigrants (34%)
- 60%+ of social assistance households are food insecure
- Most food-insecure people don't use food banks

## Key Takeaways

### Critical Concerns
1. **Debt Crisis**: All debt metrics (credit card, household, national) accelerating
2. **Social Challenges**: Homelessness and food insecurity at record highs
3. **Economic Pressures**: Real wages not keeping pace with cost increases
4. **Inequality**: Wealth concentration continues (top 20% hold 68% of financial assets)

### Positive Notes
1. **Infrastructure Investment**: Record levels of planned investment
2. **Trade Balance**: Maintaining positive trade balance overall
3. **Employment**: Some recovery in employment rates (though challenges remain)

### Structural Issues
- Housing affordability crisis driving multiple challenges
- Income supports insufficient for low-income households
- Wealth gap widening between rich and poor
- Regional disparities (especially North, Alberta, Ontario)

## Methodology Notes

### Per-Second Calculations
- Based on annual changes divided by 31,536,000 seconds per year
- Represents average rates, not actual real-time changes
- Intended to illustrate scale and pace of change
- Some metrics (like unemployment) fluctuate daily, so rates are approximations

### Data Limitations
- Some 2025 data is projected or estimated
- "Public well-being" spending lacks unified tracking
- Point-in-time homeless counts underestimate total annual homelessness
- Food insecurity surveys exclude reserves, territories (partially), and homeless populations
- Per capita calculations use estimated population figures
